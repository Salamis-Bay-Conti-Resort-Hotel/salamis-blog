Bellapais Monastery Guide: Its History, Views, and Visiting Hours

Are you looking for a spot that feels like it belongs in a fairy tale? If you are planning a trip to the northern part of the island, the Cyprus, Bellapais area is a destination you really cannot skip. The monastery is easily one of the most beautiful and peaceful places you will ever set foot in during your travels. Perched high up in the mountains with a view that stretches all the way to the deep blue sea, this ancient building is much more than just a pile of old stones, and it carries a very calm spirit.

Where is Bellapais Monastery Located?

Finding this gem is part of the fun. The monastery is tucked away in the mountains, sitting right above the coastal town of Kyrenia. Specifically, it is located in the charming Bellapais monastery village, which is famous for its narrow streets and houses covered in bright pink bougainvillea flowers. When you arrive in Bellapais, Cyprus, you will notice the air feels a little cooler and fresher than it does down by the beach. That is because of the elevation.

How to Get to Bellapais Monastery?

Reaching the Bellapais monastery is a breeze, especially if you have a rental car for the short, scenic drive up the winding mountain roads from Kyrenia. If you’d rather not drive, you can easily hop in a taxi or join a local tour group, as pretty much everyone on the island knows the way to this famous spot. Once you arrive at the village parking area, it is just a quick, lovely stroll through the traditional stone streets until you find yourself right at the monastery gates.

The History of Bellapais Monastery

The history here is a beautiful mix of the many cultures that have shaped the island over time. Looking at the architecture in Bellapais, Cyprus, you are actually seeing one of the best examples of Gothic style in the entire Middle East. It all began in the 12th century with monks from Jerusalem who were looking for a peaceful escape, and later, French Lusignan kings poured money into the site to make it truly grand. You can still see their touch in the soaring arches and the massive dining hall, where the stone carvings remain incredibly clear and detailed even centuries later.

The Story of Bellapais Monastery

The name "Bellapais" actually comes from the French phrase "Abbaye de la Paix," which means the Abbey of Peace. This tells you everything you need to know about the vibe of this place. The story of Cyprus, Bellapais is not just about kings and monks; it is about the feeling of the building itself. Legend says that the monks who lived here wore white robes, which made them earn the nickname "The White Canons."

If you want to plan your visit, it is good to know that the monastery is open almost every day of the year. During the summer months, you can usually visit from 8:00 AM until 7:00 PM. In the winter, it closes a bit earlier, usually around 5:00 PM, because the sun sets sooner. The best time to go is either early in the morning before the midday sun gets too hot or in the late afternoon. If you go late in the day, you get to see the stones of the monastery turn a beautiful golden color as the sun sets. It makes the whole experience feel even more magical. Plus, the village cafes are great for a sundowner after you finish your tour.

Is Bellapais Monastery Paid?

Yes, there is a small entrance fee to get inside the monastery grounds. The price is very reasonable and goes toward keeping the site clean and preserved for future visitors. Once you pay the fee at the entrance, you are free to explore the church, the refectory, the cloisters, and the rooftop area. Make sure to keep your ticket, and remember that it is well worth the small cost to be able to walk through such a well-preserved piece of history.

Interesting Facts About Bellapais Monastery

There are a few things that make Bellapais, Cyprus stand out from other historical sites. One of the most famous facts is about the English writer Lawrence Durrell. He lived in this village in the 1950s and wrote a very famous book called "Bitter Lemons of Cyprus." He spent a lot of time sitting under a tree in the village square, which he named the "Tree of Idleness." Legend says that anyone who sits under this tree becomes too lazy to do any work!

Another interesting feature is the two massive stone sarcophagi at the entrance of the dining hall. The monks used these as basins to wash their hands before they went in to eat. Also, if you look closely at the walls, you can see old coats of arms from the royal families of the past. The acoustics in the refectory are so good that the monastery now hosts classical music festivals every year. Imagine hearing a grand piano playing inside a 13th-century stone hall; it is an incredible experience.
